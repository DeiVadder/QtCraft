## QtCraft

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-basics"></a>Getting started

**What to do**

QtCraft is a world of blocks. Collect them, build with them, craft tools, smelt ores and stay alive: creatures come out at night and hunger grows while you work.

**The first day**

- Break a few logs of a tree with your hand (hold the left mouse button).
- Open the inventory (E) and turn the logs into planks, then planks into sticks and a crafting table.
- Place the crafting table and use it (right mouse button) to make a wooden pickaxe.
- Mine stone for a stone pickaxe and look for coal: coal and a stick make torches.
- Build a shelter before the night; a day lasts 600 s.

**This handbook**

Press H / F1 at any time. Every block, item, creature and biome has a page with its values, its recipes and links to where it comes from and what it is used for. In the inventory, the furnace and chests, point at an item and press H / F1 or click the small ? on it.

**See also**

[Oak log](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-oak_log), [Crafting table](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-crafting_table), [Wooden pickaxe](tools.md#item-qtcraft-wooden_pickaxe), [Torch](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-torch)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-controls"></a>Controls

**Keys and mouse**

- **Mouse**: look around (click into the window first), or **↑ ← ↓ →**
- **W A S D**: walk, **Space**: jump or swim up, **Shift**: sneak, **Ctrl**: sprint
- **Left mouse** or **F**: mine, attack a creature
- **Right mouse** or **R**: place, use, eat
- **Q**: throw one item, **Ctrl+Q**: the whole stack
- **1–9**, mouse wheel: choose the hotbar slot
- **E**: inventory, **T / Enter**: chat, **/**: command, **V / F5**: camera view, **H / F1**: handbook, **I / F3**: debug display (off, compact, full), **O / F4**: debug options, **Esc**: menu
- Every key except Esc can be changed under Settings → Controls.

**Mac keyboards**

On a Mac the keys F1–F12 control the brightness, Mission Control, the search and other system features, so the game does not receive them. Every action therefore has a letter key, for example **V** for the camera view and **H** for the handbook pages. To use the function keys in the game, hold fn or switch on “Use F1, F2, etc. keys as standard function keys” in the system settings (Keyboard → Keyboard Shortcuts → Function Keys). No key of the game uses Cmd, and keys pressed with Cmd do nothing in the game, because macOS keeps Cmd+Q, Cmd+H, Cmd+W, Cmd+Space and Cmd+Tab for itself: sprint is Option on a Mac, and Control+Q throws the whole stack. If you put an action on Control, note that Control with the arrow keys or Space switches desktops or the input source. Option+E, U, N and I are dead keys only while typing text; in the world they work as sprint plus that key.

**Moving**

You walk at 4.3 m/s, sprint at 5.6 m/s and sneak at 1.3 m/s; sneaking keeps you from falling off edges. Steps up to 0.5 m are climbed without jumping. In water you swim at 2.2 m/s.

**Screens**

In the inventory, furnace and chest screens a left click picks a stack up and puts it down (merging or swapping), a right click puts down a single item. The list on the right of the inventory crafts recipes directly.

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-mining"></a>Mining

**Breaking blocks**

Hold the left mouse button on a block within 5 m. Each hardness point takes 1.5 s by hand; the right tool divides that time by its speed. Broken blocks drop their items, which you pick up by walking over them.

**Tools and levels**

Stone and ores need a pickaxe of a certain level: wood is level 1, stone 2, iron 3, diamond 4. With a weaker tool or none, mining takes 5 s per hardness point and drops nothing. Tools wear out with every block.

**See also**

[Wooden pickaxe](tools.md#item-qtcraft-wooden_pickaxe), [Stone pickaxe](tools.md#item-qtcraft-stone_pickaxe), [Iron pickaxe](tools.md#item-qtcraft-iron_pickaxe), [Diamond pickaxe](tools.md#item-qtcraft-diamond_pickaxe), [Iron ore](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-iron_ore), [Diamond ore](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-diamond_ore)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-building"></a>Building

**Placing blocks**

Use the held block on a face (right mouse button) to place it next to that face. Stairs and furnaces turn by your view, slabs and stairs go high or low by where you click, torches and ladders hang on the face you click. Tall grass is replaced by what you place into it.

**Support**

Torches, ladders, lamps, doors and plants need something to stand or hang on. When it goes, they break and drop. Sand and gravel fall when nothing is below them.

**Building parts**

Many materials come as stairs, slabs, walls, fences and gates. Fences, walls and panes connect to their neighbours. Doors, trapdoors and gates open and close when used; sneak to place a block against them instead.

**See also**

[Oak stairs](parts.md#block-qtcraft-oak_stairs), [Cobblestone wall](parts.md#block-qtcraft-cobblestone_wall), [Oak fence](parts.md#block-qtcraft-oak_fence), [Oak door](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-oak_door), [Ladder](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-ladder), [Torch](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-torch)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-crafting"></a>Crafting

**The crafting grid**

The inventory has a 2 × 2 grid; a crafting table within 5 m opens 3 × 3. Lay out the ingredients as the recipe shows (shaped recipes may sit anywhere in the grid and be mirrored, shapeless ones in any order) and take the result. Leaving the screen puts the grid back into the inventory.

**Recipe list and groups**

The inventory lists what you can make right now; a click crafts it. Some recipes ask for a group such as any planks or coal or charcoal: every item of the group fits.

**See also**

[Crafting table](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-crafting_table), [Stick](items.md#item-qtcraft-stick), [Oak planks](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-oak_planks)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-smelting"></a>Furnace and fuel

**Smelting**

A furnace takes something to smelt on top and fuel below; the result appears on the right. It burns one fuel item at a time while it has work, and keeps working while you are away as long as its area is loaded. Breaking it drops what is inside.

**See also**

[Furnace](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-furnace), [Coal](items.md#item-qtcraft-coal), [Charcoal](items.md#item-qtcraft-charcoal), [Iron ingot](items.md#item-qtcraft-iron_ingot)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-hunger"></a>Food and hunger

**Eating**

The food bar holds 20 points. Hold food and use it (right mouse button) to eat when you are not full. Walking, sprinting, swimming, jumping, mining, fighting and being hurt make you hungry: every 4 points of exhaustion cost one food point. Walking empties a full bar in about 124 min, sprinting in about 8 min; jumps, mining and fights cost a little extra. Healing one health point costs 0.5 food points.

**Healing and starving**

With at least 18 food points you heal one health point every 4 s. With an empty food bar you lose one health point every 4 s, but hunger alone never takes you below 1.

**See also**

[Cooked porkchop](food.md#item-qtcraft-cooked_porkchop), [Raw porkchop](food.md#item-qtcraft-raw_porkchop), [Pig](creatures.md#mob-qtcraft-pig)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-health"></a>Health and damage

**Health**

You have 20 health points, shown as hearts. Falls deeper than 3 m cost one point per further metre; landing in water or on a ladder is safe. Hostile creatures, lava and starving hurt as well. After a hit you cannot be hurt for 0.5 s.

**Fighting**

Left-click a creature within 4 m to hit it, at most every 0.4 s. Swords and axes deal more damage than the hand; every hit knocks the target back.

**See also**

[Iron sword](tools.md#item-qtcraft-iron_sword), [Zombie](creatures.md#mob-qtcraft-zombie), [Lava](liquids.md#block-qtcraft-lava)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-death"></a>Death and respawn

**When you die**

At zero health you die. Respawn takes you back to the spawn point with full health and food. With the game rule keepInventory (on by default) you keep your items; otherwise they scatter where you died.

**Dropped items**

Items on the ground vanish after 300 s while their area is loaded. Items you throw can be picked up again after 2 s.

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-creatures"></a>Creatures

**Where creatures appear**

Creatures appear 20–48 m away from players: peaceful animals on grass by day, hostile ones in the dark at night and in caves at least 8 blocks below the surface. Torches and lamps keep hostile creatures away. Each page lists the blocks, biomes, time and light a creature needs.

**Limits**

Near each player there are at most 8 hostile, 6 peaceful and 10 ambient creatures. Hostile and ambient creatures farther than 96 m from every player vanish. Peaceful animals stay: about one in ten new chunks brings a group of them, they wait while their area is not loaded and are saved with the world. New ones only appear near you while no animal is around.

**See also**

[Zombie](creatures.md#mob-qtcraft-zombie), [Slime](creatures.md#mob-qtcraft-slime), [Pig](creatures.md#mob-qtcraft-pig), [Sheep](creatures.md#mob-qtcraft-sheep), [Firefly](creatures.md#mob-qtcraft-firefly)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-world"></a>The world

**Day and night**

A full day lasts 600 s. The world is generated from its seed while you explore it; only what you change is saved.

**Water and lava**

Water and lava flow from their sources; a bucket takes a source and pours it elsewhere. Where lava meets water it hardens into obsidian or cobblestone. Lava lights its surroundings, burns items and hurts.

**See also**

[Water](liquids.md#block-qtcraft-water), [Lava](liquids.md#block-qtcraft-lava), [Bucket](items.md#item-qtcraft-bucket), [Obsidian](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-obsidian)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-rules"></a>Game rules

**Game rules**

**keepInventory**: keep the inventory on death (chosen when creating a world). **daylightCycle**: the time of day runs. **mobSpawning**: creatures appear by themselves. Show a rule with `/gamerule <rule>`; operators change it with `/gamerule <rule> true|false`. The chapter Commands lists every command.

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-commands"></a>Commands

**Using commands**

Open the chat with T (or /) and type a command starting with `/`. `/help` lists the commands you may use, `/help <command>` shows how to use one. `~` stands for your position in coordinates (`~2` is two blocks further). A server without a window (`qtcraft-headless --console`) reads the same commands from its console, there without the leading `/`; every command is written to the server log with who ran it.

**Operators and confirmations**

Most commands are for **operators**. In single player you are always an operator; on a server, operators make others operators with `/op <player>` and take the rights away with `/deop`. Every player may use `/help`, `/list`, `/locatebiome`, reading a game rule, `/confirm` and `/cancel`. Commands that destroy a lot wait for `/confirm` (within 30 s; `/cancel` drops them): `/regenerate`, `/fill` and `/clear` of more than 4096 blocks, `/remove` of more than 16 entities, of armour stands or in the whole world.

**Players**

- `/list`: who is online
- `/locatebiome <biome>`: where the nearest place of a biome is
- `/kick <player> [reason]`: disconnect a player (he may come back)
- `/ban <player> [reason]`, `/unban <player>`, `/banlist`: keep a name out of this world
- `/whitelist on|off|list|add <player>|remove <player>`: with the whitelist on, only listed players and operators join; a ban holds for operators too
- `/op [player]`, `/deop <player>`: operator rights; `/op` alone lists the operators
- `/tp <x> <y> <z>`, `/tp <player>`, `/tp <player> <x> <y> <z>`, `/tp <player> <target player>`: teleport
Operators, bans and the whitelist are saved with the world. The host of a world cannot be kicked, banned or lose his rights.

**Repairing and cleaning up**

- `/regenerate [<x> <z> [<x2> <z2>]]`: generate the chunk at your feet (or the chunks of an area, at most 64) again as the world type makes it; every change in it is lost, chests and furnaces in it lose their contents
- `/remove <mobs|hostile|animals|ambient|items|stands|all|<creature>|item:<item>> [radius|world]`: remove entities within 32 m (at most 256 m) or in the whole loaded world; `all` means creatures and items
- `/fill <x1> <y1> <z1> <x2> <y2> <z2> <block> [replace <block>]` and `/clear <x1> <y1> <z1> <x2> <y2> <z2> [block]`: fill a box or turn it into air, at most 32768 blocks; `replace` and the block of `/clear` limit the change to one kind of block
- `/setblock <x> <y> <z> <block>`
- `/save`: save now; `/backup [label]`: save and copy the world folder into the folder backups next to the worlds, named with date and time
- `/worldinfo`: loaded chunks, entities, tick times and memory

**Time and ticks**

- `/time [set day|night|<seconds>]`, `/time add <seconds>`
- `/gamerule <keepInventory|daylightCycle|mobSpawning> [true|false]`
- `/gamemode [survival] [player]`: so far there is only survival
- `/tick freeze` and `/tick unfreeze`: the world stands still (time of day, creatures, liquids, plants, machines, hunger), players keep moving; `/tick step [ticks]` moves a frozen world on
- `/tick rate <1..100>` or `/tick rate reset` (20 per second): how fast the world runs
- `/tick sprint <ticks|seconds s|minutes min|hours h|days d>`: run that much game time as fast as possible (`/tick sprint stop` ends it)
- `/tick query`: rate, measured rate, freezing and the time per tick phase
Rate and freezing are not saved; a world always opens normally.

**Items, creatures and armour**

- `/give <item> [count]`, `/spawnitem <item> [count]`
- `/summon <creature> [count]` (at most 64 creatures), `/kill`, `/heal`, `/feed [points]`
- `/equip <set|armour|clear>` puts on armour (`/equip iron`)
- `/summon armor_stand [property=value …]` and `/armorstand <nearest|looking|id> <property> [values]` place and change armour stands. Properties: `pose <head|body|left_arm|right_arm|left_leg|right_leg> <x> <y> <z>` (degrees), `rotation <degrees>`, `arms`, `plate`, `small`, `showname` with `true|false`, `name [text]`, `equip <set|item>`, `cape <cape|none>`, `clear`, `reset`; `info` lists them. Stand ids are only valid until the world is loaded again; scripts use `nearest` or `looking`. In `/summon` values follow `=` separated by commas: `/summon armor_stand equip=iron pose=right_arm,90,0,0 small`.

**Commands from mods**

Mods add commands as macros (`commands/*.json`): a name and command lines that run one after another with your rights, so a macro never lets you do more than you may do anyway. `/help` lists them with the others.
