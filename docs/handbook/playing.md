## QtCraft

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-basics"></a>Getting started

**What to do**

QtCraft is a world of blocks. Collect them, build with them, craft tools, smelt ores and stay alive: creatures come out at night and hunger grows while you work.

**The first day**

- Break a few logs of a tree with your hand (hold the left mouse button).
- Open the inventory (E) and turn the logs into planks, then planks into sticks and a crafting table.
- Place the crafting table and use it (right mouse button) to make a wooden pickaxe.
- Mine stone for a stone pickaxe and look for coal: coal and a stick make torches.
- Build a shelter before the night; a day lasts 600 s.

**This handbook**

Press H / F1 at any time. Every block, item, creature and biome has a page with its values, its recipes and links to where it comes from and what it is used for. In the inventory, the furnace and chests, point at an item and press H / F1 or click the small ? on it.

**See also**

[Oak log](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-oak_log), [Crafting table](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-crafting_table), [Wooden pickaxe](tools.md#item-qtcraft-wooden_pickaxe), [Torch](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-torch)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-controls"></a>Controls

**Keys and mouse**

- **Mouse**: look around (click into the window first), or **↑ ← ↓ →**
- **W A S D**: walk, **Space**: jump or swim up, **Shift**: sneak, **Ctrl**: sprint
- **Left mouse** or **F**: mine, attack a creature
- **Right mouse** or **R**: place, use, eat
- **Middle mouse** or **B**: pick the block you look at
- **Q**: throw one item, **Ctrl+Q**: the whole stack
- **1–9**, mouse wheel: choose the hotbar slot
- **E**: inventory, **T / Enter**: chat, **/**: command, **V / F5**: camera view, **H / F1**: handbook, **I / F3**: debug display (off, compact, full), **O / F4**: debug options, **Esc**: menu
- Every key except Esc can be changed under Settings → Controls.

**Mac keyboards**

On a Mac the keys F1–F12 control the brightness, Mission Control, the search and other system features, so the game does not receive them. Every action therefore has a letter key, for example **V** for the camera view and **H** for the handbook pages. To use the function keys in the game, hold fn or switch on “Use F1, F2, etc. keys as standard function keys” in the system settings (Keyboard → Keyboard Shortcuts → Function Keys). No key of the game uses Cmd, and keys pressed with Cmd do nothing in the game, because macOS keeps Cmd+Q, Cmd+H, Cmd+W, Cmd+Space and Cmd+Tab for itself: sprint is Option on a Mac, and Control+Q throws the whole stack. If you put an action on Control, note that Control with the arrow keys or Space switches desktops or the input source. Option+E, U, N and I are dead keys only while typing text; in the world they work as sprint plus that key.

**Moving**

You walk at 4.3 m/s, sprint at 5.6 m/s and sneak at 1.3 m/s; sneaking keeps you from falling off edges. To sprint, press **Ctrl** while walking forward or tap **W** twice; the sprint lasts as long as you keep walking forward. The view widens a little while you sprint (Settings → FOV effects). Steps up to 0.5 m are climbed without jumping. In water you swim at 2.2 m/s.

**Screens**

In the inventory, furnace and chest screens a left click picks a stack up and puts it down (merging or swapping), a right click puts down a single item. The list on the right of the inventory crafts recipes directly.

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-mining"></a>Mining

**Breaking blocks**

Hold the left mouse button on a block within 5 m. Each hardness point takes 1.5 s by hand; the right tool divides that time by its speed. Broken blocks drop their items, which you pick up by walking over them.

**Tools and levels**

Stone and ores need a pickaxe of a certain level: wood is level 1, stone 2, iron 3, diamond 4. With a weaker tool or none, mining takes 5 s per hardness point and drops nothing. Tools wear out with every block.

**See also**

[Wooden pickaxe](tools.md#item-qtcraft-wooden_pickaxe), [Stone pickaxe](tools.md#item-qtcraft-stone_pickaxe), [Iron pickaxe](tools.md#item-qtcraft-iron_pickaxe), [Diamond pickaxe](tools.md#item-qtcraft-diamond_pickaxe), [Iron ore](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-iron_ore), [Diamond ore](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-diamond_ore)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-building"></a>Building

**Placing blocks**

Use the held block on a face (right mouse button) to place it next to that face. Stairs and furnaces turn by your view, slabs and stairs go high or low by where you click, torches and ladders hang on the face you click. Tall grass is replaced by what you place into it.

**Support**

Torches, ladders, lamps, doors and plants need something to stand or hang on. When it goes, they break and drop. Sand and gravel fall when nothing is below them.

**Building parts**

Many materials come as stairs, slabs, walls, fences and gates. Fences, walls and panes connect to their neighbours. Doors, trapdoors and gates open and close when used; sneak to place a block against them instead.

**See also**

[Oak stairs](parts.md#block-qtcraft-oak_stairs), [Cobblestone wall](parts.md#block-qtcraft-cobblestone_wall), [Oak fence](parts.md#block-qtcraft-oak_fence), [Oak door](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-oak_door), [Ladder](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-ladder), [Torch](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-torch)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-crafting"></a>Crafting

**The crafting grid**

The inventory has a 2 × 2 grid; a crafting table within 5 m opens 3 × 3. Lay out the ingredients as the recipe shows (shaped recipes may sit anywhere in the grid and be mirrored, shapeless ones in any order) and take the result. Leaving the screen puts the grid back into the inventory.

**Recipe list and groups**

The inventory lists what you can make right now; a click crafts it. Some recipes ask for a group such as any planks or coal or charcoal: every item of the group fits.

**See also**

[Crafting table](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-crafting_table), [Stick](items.md#item-qtcraft-stick), [Oak planks](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-oak_planks)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-smelting"></a>Furnace and fuel

**Smelting**

A furnace takes something to smelt on top and fuel below; the result appears on the right. It burns one fuel item at a time while it has work, and keeps working while you are away as long as its area is loaded. Breaking it drops what is inside.

**See also**

[Furnace](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-furnace), [Coal](items.md#item-qtcraft-coal), [Charcoal](items.md#item-qtcraft-charcoal), [Iron ingot](items.md#item-qtcraft-iron_ingot)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-hunger"></a>Food and hunger

**Eating**

The food bar holds 20 points. Hold food and use it (right mouse button) to eat when you are not full. Walking, sprinting, swimming, jumping, mining, fighting and being hurt make you hungry: every 4 points of exhaustion cost one food point. Walking empties a full bar in about 124 min, sprinting in about 8 min; jumps, mining and fights cost a little extra. Healing one health point costs 0.5 food points.

**Healing and starving**

With at least 18 food points you heal one health point every 4 s. With an empty food bar you lose one health point every 4 s, but hunger alone never takes you below 1.

**See also**

[Cooked porkchop](food.md#item-qtcraft-cooked_porkchop), [Raw porkchop](food.md#item-qtcraft-raw_porkchop), [Pig](creatures.md#mob-qtcraft-pig)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-farming"></a>Farming

**Farmland**

Use a hoe on grass or dirt with air above it to make farmland; every tilled block wears the hoe once (not in creative mode). Water within four blocks around it, at the same height or one above, keeps farmland wet. Without water it dries out; dry farmland without a crop on it turns back into dirt, and so does farmland under a full block. Walking and jumping on farmland do not trample it.

**Crops**

Plant wheat seeds, carrots or potatoes on farmland. Crops grow in stages on random ticks (each block gets one about every 68 s) while their chunk is loaded; time while the world is closed does not count. They grow twice as fast on wet farmland and need light of at least 9, from the sky or from lamps. Break a ripe crop for the harvest; an unripe one only gives back what was planted. Seeds come from tall grass. Growing wheat, carrots and potatoes are found only in the fields of villages: harvest a ripe plant there for your first carrots and potatoes (a zombie now and then drops one too).

**Pumpkins and melons**

Pumpkin and melon seeds grow into stems. A ripe stem puts its fruit onto a free neighbouring block of farmland, dirt or grass, one at a time; harvest the fruit and the stem grows another. Leave a free side next to every stem. A pumpkin gives four seeds, a melon slice one; pumpkins and melons also grow wild.

**Seeds plant themselves**

Seeds, carrots and potatoes lying on free farmland plant one of their stack by themselves (checked every 1 s, with a high chance). A stack whose own block is taken, or that holds more, plants into the free farmland right around it, one seed per check, so drops that merged into one stack still plant the whole patch. Flowing water carries dropped items along its current; still water does not. A field washed off with water from behind replants itself where the drops come to rest; collect the rest of the harvest.

**Breeding animals**

Feed pigs with carrots or potatoes and sheep with wheat: a fed adult falls in love for a while and walks towards another one of its kind in love; two that see each other get a young one. Parents rest five minutes before they breed again; a young one grows up in twenty minutes, each feeding takes 10 % of that time off. Young animals leave nothing behind. Crowded places get no more young ones.

**Food from the farm**

Bread from three wheat, baked potatoes from the furnace, pumpkin pie from a pumpkin, sugar and wheat, melon slices and carrots to eat on the way. Carrots and potatoes are planted when used on farmland and eaten elsewhere.

**See also**

[Wooden hoe](tools.md#item-qtcraft-wooden_hoe), [Farmland](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-farmland), [Wheat seeds](items.md#item-qtcraft-wheat_seeds), [Wheat crop](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-wheat_crop), [Carrot](food.md#item-qtcraft-carrot), [Potato](food.md#item-qtcraft-potato), [Pumpkin stem](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-pumpkin_stem), [Melon stem](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-melon_stem), [Bread](food.md#item-qtcraft-bread), [Pig](creatures.md#mob-qtcraft-pig), [Sheep](creatures.md#mob-qtcraft-sheep)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-signal"></a>Signal Dust

**The wire**

Signal Dust comes from Signal ore deep below (an iron pickaxe or better). Laid on a block it becomes a wire: it connects to the dust next to it, climbs up the side of a block when nothing opaque lies on it, and runs down the side of a block that is not opaque. A lone dust is a dot that powers all four sides; one connection makes a straight line that points on; more make corners and crosses. A signal starts with strength 15 and loses one per block of wire, so after fifteen blocks it is gone; a Signal Repeater starts it at 15 again.

**Powered blocks**

Full solid blocks like stone carry power on. A lever or button powers the block it hangs on strongly, a plate the block below, a Signal Torch the block above, a repeater the block in front. A strongly powered block powers dust and slots around it. Dust powers the block it lies on and the blocks its lines point at only weakly: a weakly powered block still powers lamps, pistons, doors, torches and repeaters next to it, but never other dust. Glass, pistons and slabs carry nothing.

**Sources**

A lever stays on or off. A stone button gives a signal for one second, a wooden one for one and a half. A wooden pressure plate is pressed by anything lying or standing on it, a stone one only by players and creatures; it springs back about a second after it is free. A Signal Torch is on unless its block is powered, two ticks after the change: an inverter. Switched on and off more than eight times within three seconds it burns out for eight seconds. A Signal Repeater passes a signal from back to front after one to four steps of two ticks (use it to change the delay); a short pulse comes out as long as its delay, and a powered repeater pointing at its side locks it.

**Slots**

What reacts to a signal is called a slot. A Signal Lamp lights at once and goes dark a moment after it loses power. Doors, trapdoors and fence gates open when their power comes and close when it goes; in between players still open and close them by hand. Iron doors and iron trapdoors open only by signal, not by hand.

**Pistons**

A powered Signal Piston pushes up to 12 blocks in front of it by one. Chests, barrels and furnaces move with everything in them. Plants, torches, dust and liquids in the way break; bedrock, obsidian and extended pistons stop it. Players, creatures, items and armour stands in the way are pushed along. A Sticky Signal Piston pulls the block in front of its head back when it retracts. A piston is not powered through its front.

**Hoppers**

A Signal Hopper moves 2.5 items a second: it passes one item from its first slot that fits into the container its spout points at, then takes one from the container above it. Without a container above it collects items lying in its bowl or on the block above it, also through a slab, a carpet, farmland or any block that does not fill its cell; under a full block it collects nothing. Into a furnace it puts items from above into the input and from the side into the fuel slot (fuel only); from below it takes the furnace's output. Any signal locks it. A hopper with nothing to do rests until something around it changes, so idle hoppers cost nothing. Several hoppers that put items into one full container at the same time: the one from above first, then the others by item, equal items all or none.

**Comparators**

A Signal Comparator passes on the strength from its back, two ticks later. A chest, barrel, hopper, dispenser or furnace behind it (also behind one full block) gives its fullness instead: nothing in it is 0, otherwise 1 plus 14 times how full its slots are on average. Ripe wheat, carrots and potatoes are read too (0 to 15 by their stage). In compare mode a stronger side input (dust, a repeater or comparator pointing at it) switches it off; in subtract mode the side input is taken off. Use it to switch the mode. A hopper sorter whose filter is read by a comparator needs an inverter to lock the hopper below the filter: a Signal Torch burns out when one kind of item comes at full hopper speed (eight times off within three seconds) and the filter then drains; a comparator in subtract mode with a lever behind it does not burn out (the showcase's sorter).

**Observers**

A Signal Observer watches the block in front of its face. When that block changes its state (placed, broken, a crop grows, dust changes its strength, a door opens), it sends a pulse of two ticks out of its back, two ticks later. Changes of what is inside a container do not count. Two observers looking at each other make a fast clock.

**Dispensers and droppers**

When their power comes, a Signal Dispenser and a Signal Dropper eject one item from a random slot four ticks later (powered like a piston, also through the block above). A dropper facing a container puts the item into it, otherwise it throws it out. A dispenser pours water and lava buckets, takes a liquid with an empty bucket, puts armour on a player or armour stand in front and throws everything else out, also in front of a container. Seeds, carrots and potatoes are thrown, never planted: they plant themselves when they land on free farmland.

**Known quirks**

Pistons are also powered by whatever would power the block above them, but only notice it when a block next to them changes: a piston can sit powered and still until then (a block update detector, BUD). The game rule quasiConnectivity switches this off for a world; a piston can refuse it in its data. A pulse shorter than a piston's two-tick extension (down to one that comes and goes within a single tick) pushes the block and lets go of it: a sticky piston leaves its block one further (block spitting). Torch clocks burn out, and repeaters keep their delays.

**Same in every place**

A circuit behaves the same at every coordinate and in every rotation. Everything that switches in the same step decides on the world as it was before the step and switches together. When two pistons want the same block or cell in the same step, both stay where they are.

**On a server**

Circuits run on the server; everyone sees the same states. Levers, buttons and repeaters switch at most 5 times a second per player. The work of all circuits per tick is limited; a huge machine slows down instead of stopping the server.

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-rails"></a>Rails and Minecarts

**Laying track**

Rails need a solid block below them. A rail joins the rails beside it on its own: one neighbour makes a straight piece that points at it, two at a right angle make a curve, a line of three or four keeps the straight line (a branch does not bend it). A rail with a rail one block higher beside it rises towards it, so a track climbs and descends one block at a time. Where the neighbours decide nothing, a new rail runs the way you look. Rails behave the same in every direction.

**Minecarts**

Use a minecart on a rail to set it down. A cart keeps rolling along the track, round curves and over slopes: downhill it speeds up, uphill it slows down and may roll back, and it slowly loses speed on the flat (a ridden cart much more slowly than an empty one). At the end of a track it stops. Carts that run into each other pass their speed on; walking into a cart pushes it. A cart without rails under it slides to a stop. One hit breaks a cart; it drops itself.

**Riding**

Use a cart to get in and sneak to get out; you land beside it. While you ride, the forward key pushes the cart along the way you look. The server moves the cart and its rider, so everyone sees you ride the same way.

**Pistons and carts**

A Signal Piston pushes a cart in front of its head or in front of the block it moves, like any other body; a piston pushing a line of rails moves the rails and the cart on them. A rider stays in the cart.

**Powered, detector and activator rails**

Three rails work with Signal Dust. A powered rail speeds a cart up while it gets power, up to the top speed, and brakes it to a stop while it does not; a cart standing on a powered rail with a block at one end starts away from the block. A detector rail gives a signal while a cart is on it; a Signal Comparator behind it reads the contents of a chest or hopper cart on it. An activator rail with power puts the rider out and stops a hopper cart pulling; without power it lets hopper carts pull again. Power given to one powered or activator rail passes along the line of rails of the same kind for eight rails, also up and down slopes, and goes when the power goes. These rails do not bend: where two rails meet at a corner they keep their line. Everything works the same in every direction.

**Chest and hopper carts**

A chest cart carries a chest: use it to open its screen like a chest. A hopper cart carries a Signal Hopper and pulls one item at a time from a container above its rail, or picks up items lying on its rail; it rests between items like a hopper. A Signal Hopper under a rail takes the items out of a chest or hopper cart standing above it, one at a time, and passes them on; Signal Hoppers can carry rails. Broken, a cart drops itself and what it carried. Carts keep their contents when their chunk is saved or unloaded.

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-health"></a>Health and damage

**Health**

You have 20 health points, shown as hearts. Falls deeper than 3 m cost one point per further metre; landing in water or on a ladder is safe. Hostile creatures, lava and starving hurt as well. After a hit you cannot be hurt for 0.5 s.

**Fighting**

Left-click a creature within 4 m to hit it, at most every 0.4 s. Swords and axes deal more damage than the hand; every hit knocks the target back.

**See also**

[Iron sword](tools.md#item-qtcraft-iron_sword), [Zombie](creatures.md#mob-qtcraft-zombie), [Lava](liquids.md#block-qtcraft-lava)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-fire"></a>Fire

**Burning**

Fire stands on a solid block or next to a flammable one, and it lights everything around it with light 15. About every 1.5 to 1.95 seconds it takes a step and grows older; after 45 steps at the latest it goes out. Without a flammable block next to it, it goes out after 9 steps, so fire on stone or sand burns down quickly. Fire whose ground or fuel goes away goes out at once, and water flowing in washes it away. Breaking fire puts it out.

**Lighting fire**

Flint and steel lights fire in front of the face of a block you use it on, if fire can stand there: on a solid block or next to a flammable one. Each fire wears it by one use; in creative mode it does not wear. A Signal Dispenser holding flint and steel lights the cell in front of it when it fires; if nothing can burn there, the flint and steel stays inside. Lava now and then sets fire to the air right next to it when that air touches a flammable block, but only when something next to the lava changes or it flows: a still lava lake does not set the woods around it alight. Every new fire is a spark of its own and keeps the limits below. Breaking or punching fire puts it out, also in creative mode.

**Spreading**

Each step may burn away the flammable blocks next to the fire: they turn into fire or into air, and nothing drops. It may also light the air around it next to flammable blocks, most readily above it. How readily a block catches fire and how quickly it burns away depends on the block: leaves, wool, grass and flowers burn readily, logs, planks and coal blocks slowly; stone, earth, glass and metal never burn. Fire on a magma block burns forever without spreading: a hearth. A new fire is as old as the one that lit it, so a fire front always dies out.

**Limits**

Fire spreads at most 12 blocks from the spark it began with, so a single spark never burns down a whole forest or village. It never spreads into chunks that are not loaded. A chunk holds at most 256 fires and the world 4096; beyond, no new fire is lit. The game rule `fireSpread` (`/gamerule fireSpread false`) stops fire from spreading and burning blocks away; fires then only burn where they are until they go out. The showcase world has it off. A server can also switch spreading off for all its worlds with the limit `fireSpreadRadius` 0 in server.json.

**Burning players and creatures**

Whoever walks into fire or lava catches fire and keeps burning for a while after stepping out (fire 8 seconds, lava 15; see the block pages). While burning, a player or creature loses 1 health every 1 second; armour takes part of it like any blow. Water puts the flames out at once, and so does rain falling on you. Players in creative mode do not burn. Items that fall into fire or lava burn up.

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-weather"></a>Weather

**Clear, rain and thunderstorms**

The weather is the same in the whole world. A clear spell lasts 5 to 25 minutes; then it rains for 2.5 to 10 minutes, or, with a chance of 25 in 100, a thunderstorm rages for 2 to 6 minutes; after that it clears up again. Rain and thunder come and go over 10 seconds: clouds cover the sky, it grows darker, the fog comes closer and the sky lights the land less. Each world has its own course of weather, given by its seed.

**Rain and snow**

Rain and snow fall only where the sky is open: not under a roof, a tree or an overhang. In cold biomes (taiga, tundra) it snows instead of raining, and in every other biome it snows from 150 blocks up (in the mountains from their snow line). In the desert it stays dry, although the sky darkens there too. You hear the rain around you, muffled under a roof.

**Thunderstorms**

In a thunderstorm lightning strikes now and then within 48 blocks of a player, on average every 10 seconds per player, only where it rains (not into snow or the desert). The flash lights up the sky at once; the thunder follows as late as sound needs for the distance: about three seconds per kilometre. A strike hurts everyone within 3 blocks by 5 health (armour takes part of it) and sets them alight; it may light a few fires where it strikes, but only while fire spreads (`fireSpread`) and within the limits of fire.

**What rain and snow do**

Rain puts out the fires it falls on, sooner or later (the older the fire, the sooner), and fire does not spread into the rain; fire on a magma block burns on. It also puts out burning players and creatures, and it keeps farmland under the open sky wet like water nearby. It never fills the world with water. Where it snows, a thin snow cover settles bit by bit on the tops of solid blocks under the open sky, not under a roof, on ice, glass or leaves, and only one layer per spot. Snow does not melt again.

**Changing the weather**

`/weather` shows the weather. Operators set it with `/weather clear`, `/weather rain` or `/weather thunder`, for a number of seconds (`/weather rain 120`) or as long as the course of the world says. With the game rule **weatherCycle** off (`/gamerule weatherCycle false`) the weather stays as it is until it is set again; the showcase world starts that way, with a clear sky.

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-villages"></a>Villages

**Finding villages**

Villages stand on flat land in plains, deserts, taiga and tundra, and on stilts in the wet parts of swamps; never in oceans, mountains or forests. The world is divided into square regions (384 blocks in the base game), and a region holds at most one village, about half a region away from the next. Where a village goes depends only on the seed, so a world of the same seed has its villages in the same places. Operators find the nearest one with `/locate village`; `/village` shows its state.

**Styles**

Each biome builds in its own style. Plains villages are made of oak and cobblestone with pitched roofs of stairs, a well with a bell in the middle, main roads of cobblestone or gravel, side roads of dirt path and lamp posts. Desert villages are made of sandstone and terracotta with flat roofs, main roads of terracotta or packed mud that stand out against the sand, side roads of cut sandstone and fields with sugar cane by the water. Taiga villages are the plains houses in spruce. Swamp villages stand in the water on stilts of dark spruce: huts with thatched roofs, a meeting hall with the bell on a large platform in the middle, a fisher's hut, a herb hut with mushrooms and raised fields, all joined by boardwalks at one height above the water, with stairs down where a boardwalk reaches dry land. Around them the swamp is shallow water over mud. A biome that has villages but no style of its own builds in the plains style.

**Buildings**

Every village has its own street plan: one long main street through the middle with side streets, four roads from the well to all sides, three in a T or two in an L, a ring road around a square with the well, or roads at all angles that bend; some roads wind along the slopes. Diagonal roads run as staircases, and the houses at them have a forecourt to the road. Roads stop at water and steep slopes. Along them stand small and large houses with beds, fields of wheat, carrots and potatoes, ripe and still growing, with water and paths between the rows, a smithy with furnaces and a church or temple, each with its door to the road. Every kind of house comes in several designs, and no two houses need to look alike: walls of oak, birch or spruce, roofs of wood, cobblestone or brick, a chimney, flowers in front, a shed beside, terracotta in different colours in the desert. Some villages have an animal pen with a pair of pigs or sheep; a village near water has a fisher's hut with a jetty out over the water; large villages have a stone watchtower, taller than every house, with a ladder up to its battlements. Where a main road climbs a terrace by one block, a slab makes the step. Lanterns light the houses inside and lamp posts the roads, so no monsters spawn in them. Houses rest on a foundation where the ground is uneven, and the ground above them is cleared.

**Beds and workstations**

The world remembers every village: its middle, its area, its beds and its workstations (composter: farmer, furnace: smith, barrel: fisher). Beds and workstations you place or break inside a village count at once. Villagers claim a bed and a workstation each (see Villagers). A village lists at most 64 beds and 64 workstations; the server limits `villageBeds`, `villageWorkstations` and `villages` change that.

**Villages in mods**

A style is a file in `villages/`: the biomes it builds in, the well, the lamp post, the kinds of buildings with weights and maximum counts, each with one or several designs, the street plans (main street, cross, T, L, ring road, branching roads, winding or straight), the road surfaces for main roads and side roads (one chosen per village, with an optional slab for steps), the crops of the fields and the foundation block and a palette that swaps blocks of the houses. A kind can have rules: a chance per village, a smallest village size (the length of all its roads) or water within reach (then it gets a jetty). A style can stand over water on stilts, with boardwalks for roads and the swamp around it shaped into shallow water over mud. The houses are ordinary structures; `choices` (block swaps, one option per house) and optional `parts` with a chance (chimney, flowers, shed) make every house different. A mod can add a style for its own biome, a new style from the base game's houses with another palette, or new house designs.

**See also**

[Bell](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-bell), [Bed](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-bed), [Composter](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-composter), [Furnace](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-furnace), [Barrel](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-barrel), [Farmland](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-farmland), [Wheat crop](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-wheat_crop), [Lantern](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-lantern), [Plains](biomes.md#biome-qtcraft-plains), [Desert](biomes.md#biome-qtcraft-desert), [Taiga](biomes.md#biome-qtcraft-taiga), [Swamp](biomes.md#biome-qtcraft-swamp), [Mud](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-mud)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-villagers"></a>Villagers

**Homes**

When the part of a village with a bed is seen for the first time, a villager moves into every free bed, up to 32 in one village. Each one claims its bed and keeps it; break the bed and the villager looks for another free one. Villagers stay in the world for good: they wait while their part of the world is not loaded and are saved with it. Their clothes follow the style of their village: tunics on the plains, robes and headwraps in the desert, fur in the taiga, smocks and waders with reed hats in the swamp, each villager with its own colours.

**Professions**

A villager without work claims a free workstation and takes its profession: the composter makes a farmer (apron and straw hat), the furnace a smith (leather apron) and the barrel a fisher (oilskin vest and hat). Each workstation belongs to one villager. Farmers harvest the ripe crops of the village fields around their composter and plant them again at once; the harvest stays with the village (no items drop). A villager that loses its workstation before it ever traded is out of work again. Trading comes with a later version: for now a villager you use turns to you and greets you.

**A villager's day**

After sunrise villagers stroll about, then go to work. Around noon they meet at the bell and go back to work in the afternoon. Towards evening they stroll once more, at sunset they go home, and at night they lie in their beds until morning. A villager in bed gets up when it is hurt. When it rains in the village, everyone goes home. Monsters within 10 blocks send villagers running home; one that is hit runs away too. They can be hurt like animals and do not fight back.

**Doors and long ways**

Villagers open wooden doors on their way and close them behind them; iron doors stay shut for them. A long way through the village, say across the boardwalks of a swamp village, goes along the roads in stages, so villagers find their way home from every corner of their village.

**Villagers in mods**

`villagers.json` names the creature that lives in villages, the professions (the workstation kind from `worldgen/villages.json`, layers laid over the outfit, a hat, whether it tends crops), the daily routine as steps through the day (wander, work, meet, home, sleep) and the default look. A village style dresses its villagers with `villagers` in its file: an outfit, hair styles and hats to choose from and lists of skin, hair, shirt and trouser colours.

**See also**

[Villager](creatures.md#mob-qtcraft-villager), [Bed](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-bed), [Bell](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-bell), [Composter](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-composter), [Furnace](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-furnace), [Barrel](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-barrel), [Oak door](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-oak_door), [Iron door](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-iron_door), [Wheat crop](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-wheat_crop)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-death"></a>Death and respawn

**When you die**

At zero health you die. Respawn takes you back to the spawn point with full health and food. With the game rule keepInventory (on by default) you keep your items; otherwise they scatter where you died.

**The void**

Nothing holds you below the bottom of the world: through a hole in the bedrock or off the edge of an empty world you fall into the void. From 64 blocks below the bottom the void hurts you by 4 points every 0.5 s, whatever armour you wear, and in creative mode too, even while flying. Dying there is “fell out of the world”; items, creatures and anything else that falls that deep are gone for good.

**Dropped items**

Items on the ground vanish after 300 s while their area is loaded. Items you throw can be picked up again after 2 s.

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-experience"></a>Experience

**Experience orbs**

Mining ores (coal, diamond, lapis lazuli, Signal Dust ore), killing creatures, smelting in a furnace and breeding animals give experience as small glowing orbs. Orbs fly to the nearest player within 8 blocks and are collected with a chime; a furnace keeps the experience of what it smelted until a player takes the result. Orbs close together merge into one, and they vanish after 300 s while their area is loaded. A world holds at most 1024 orbs; beyond that new experience joins the nearest orb, so nothing is lost.

**Levels**

Experience fills the green bar above the hotbar; when it is full you reach the next level, shown by the number in the middle, with a fanfare. Each level costs more than the one before: 7 points for the first, 17 for level 5, 37 for level 15 and 112 for level 30. Levels are what traders and enchanting will ask for.

**Experience and death**

When you die you drop 7 points per level as orbs, at most 100 points; the rest is lost and you start again at level 0. With the game rule keepInventory you keep your experience, and so do players in creative mode.

**The /xp command**

Operators change experience with /xp add <player> <amount> [points|levels] (a negative amount takes away), /xp set <player> <amount> [points|levels] and /xp query <player>.

**See also**

[Lapis lazuli ore](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-lapis_ore), [Furnace](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-furnace)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-creatures"></a>Creatures

**Where creatures appear**

Creatures appear 20–48 m away from players: peaceful animals on grass by day, hostile ones in the dark at night and in caves at least 8 blocks below the surface. Torches and lamps keep hostile creatures away. Each page lists the blocks, biomes, time and light a creature needs.

**Limits**

Near each player there are at most 8 hostile, 6 peaceful and 10 ambient creatures. Hostile and ambient creatures farther than 96 m from every player vanish. Peaceful animals stay: about one in ten new chunks brings a group of them, they wait while their area is not loaded and are saved with the world. New ones only appear near you while no animal is around. Hostile creatures only go after players they see, and follow one they lost sight of for a few seconds. Many creatures in one block cell think less to save work: from 8 on only now and then and without looking for a way, from 24 on not at all; they still fall, are pushed apart and strike a player right next to them.

**See also**

[Zombie](creatures.md#mob-qtcraft-zombie), [Slime](creatures.md#mob-qtcraft-slime), [Pig](creatures.md#mob-qtcraft-pig), [Sheep](creatures.md#mob-qtcraft-sheep), [Firefly](creatures.md#mob-qtcraft-firefly)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-world"></a>The world

**Day and night**

A full day lasts 600 s. The world is generated from its seed while you explore it; only what you change is saved.

**Water and lava**

Water and lava flow from their sources; a bucket takes a source and pours it elsewhere. Where lava meets water it hardens into obsidian or cobblestone. Lava lights its surroundings, burns items and hurts.

**See also**

[Water](liquids.md#block-qtcraft-water), [Lava](liquids.md#block-qtcraft-lava), [Bucket](items.md#item-qtcraft-bucket), [Obsidian](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-obsidian)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-creative"></a>Creative mode

**Survival and creative**

Every player has a game mode. In **survival** you gather, craft, eat and can die. In **creative** you build freely: every block and item without limit, flying, blocks break at the first strike, nothing hurts you and you never get hungry. A new world asks for the mode of its players in the main menu (**Game mode**); operators change it with `/gamemode creative` or `/gamemode survival` (also for another player: `/gamemode creative Alice`), and `/defaultgamemode` sets the mode of players who join for the first time.

**Flying**

Tap **Space** twice quickly to take off or to stop flying. While flying, **Space** rises, **Shift** sinks and **Ctrl** doubles the speed (10.9 m/s, sprinting 21.6 m/s); the view widens a little. Sinking onto the ground lands you and ends the flight. The showcase world has a platform high above the ground that only flyers reach.

**Creative inventory**

**E** opens the creative inventory: every block and item of the game and of the loaded mods, sorted into tabs (blocks, building parts, tools and armour, food, other items) with a search that finds names in your language, English names and ids like `oak_planks`. A left click takes a full stack, a right click one item, a shift-click puts a full stack straight into the inventory; click a slot to put it there. The bin deletes a stack. **Crafting and armour** leads to the usual inventory with the crafting grid and the armour slots.

**Rules in creative mode**

- Blocks break at once (even bedrock) and drop nothing; chests and furnaces still lose their contents.
- Placing a block keeps your stack, a poured bucket stays full.
- No damage except `/kill`; creatures leave you alone; food stays as it is.
- **Middle mouse** or **B** puts the block you look at into your hand; in survival this selects it when it is in your inventory.

**Changing between the modes**

Survival and creative each have their own inventory and armour. Changing the mode puts the current ones aside and brings back those of the other mode, so creative items never end up in your survival inventory. Health and food stay as they were. Chests belong to the world: what you put into a chest in creative mode is there in survival too.

**See also**

[Crafting table](blocks.md#block-qtcraft-crafting_table)

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-rules"></a>Game rules

**Game rules**

**keepInventory**: keep the inventory on death (chosen when creating a world). **daylightCycle**: the time of day runs. **mobSpawning**: creatures appear by themselves. **randomTicks**: plants, crops and farmland change by themselves (off in the showcase world). **quasiConnectivity**: pistons also react to power at the block above them, but only after a block update next to them (on by default). **weatherCycle**: the weather changes by itself (off in the showcase world). **maxEntityCramming** (a number, 24 by default, like the reference): creatures in one block cell beyond this many take damage until no more than that are left, and drop their loot; 0 switches it off. Mob farms use it to kill what drops into a cell of one block. Show a rule with `/gamerule <rule>`; operators change it with `/gamerule <rule> true|false`. The chapter Commands lists every command.

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-commands"></a>Commands

**Using commands**

Open the chat with T (or /) and type a command starting with `/`. `/help` lists the commands you may use, `/help <command>` shows how to use one. `~` stands for your position in coordinates (`~2` is two blocks further). A server without a window (`qtcraft-headless --console`) reads the same commands from its console, there without the leading `/`; every command is written to the server log with who ran it.

**Operators and confirmations**

Most commands are for **operators**. In single player you are always an operator; on a server, operators make others operators with `/op <player>` and take the rights away with `/deop`. Every player may use `/help`, `/list`, `/locatebiome`, reading a game rule, `/confirm` and `/cancel`. Commands that destroy a lot wait for `/confirm` (within 30 s; `/cancel` drops them): `/regenerate`, `/fill` and `/clear` of more than 4096 blocks, `/remove` of more than 16 entities, of armour stands or in the whole world.

**Players**

- `/list`: who is online
- `/locatebiome <biome>`: where the nearest place of a biome is
- `/locate village`: where the nearest village is (operators); `/village [<x> <z>]`: its state as JSON, with beds and workstations
- `/kick <player> [reason]`: disconnect a player (he may come back)
- `/ban <player> [reason]`, `/unban <player>`, `/banlist`: keep a name out of this world
- `/whitelist on|off|list|add <player>|remove <player>`: with the whitelist on, only listed players and operators join; a ban holds for operators too
- `/op [player]`, `/deop <player>`: operator rights; `/op` alone lists the operators
- `/tp <x> <y> <z>`, `/tp <player>`, `/tp <player> <x> <y> <z>`, `/tp <player> <target player>`: teleport
Operators, bans and the whitelist are saved with the world. The host of a world cannot be kicked, banned or lose his rights.

**Repairing and cleaning up**

- `/regenerate [<x> <z> [<x2> <z2>]]`: generate the chunk at your feet (or the chunks of an area, at most 64) again as the world type makes it; every change in it is lost, chests and furnaces in it lose their contents
- `/remove <mobs|hostile|animals|ambient|items|stands|all|<creature>|item:<item>> [radius|world]`: remove entities within 32 m (at most 256 m) or in the whole loaded world; `all` means creatures and items
- `/fill <x1> <y1> <z1> <x2> <y2> <z2> <block> [replace <block>]` and `/clear <x1> <y1> <z1> <x2> <y2> <z2> [block]`: fill a box or turn it into air, at most 32768 blocks; `replace` and the block of `/clear` limit the change to one kind of block
- `/setblock <x> <y> <z> <block>`
- `/save`: save now; `/backup [label]`: save and copy the world folder into the folder backups next to the worlds, named with date and time
- `/worldinfo`: loaded chunks, entities, tick times and memory

**Time and ticks**

- `/time [set day|night|<seconds>]`, `/time add <seconds>`
- `/gamerule <keepInventory|daylightCycle|mobSpawning|randomTicks|fireSpread|quasiConnectivity|weatherCycle> [true|false]`; `/gamerule spawnChunkRadius [0-16]`: the chunks around the spawn that stay loaded (0 none, 1 the spawn chunk, 2 the 3 × 3 around it …); `/gamerule maxEntityCramming [0-4096]`: creatures in one block cell beyond which they take damage (0 never)
- `/weather [clear|rain|thunder] [seconds]`: shows the weather; operators set it, for that many seconds or as long as it would last
- `/gamemode [survival|creative] [player]`: shows or changes the game mode, `/defaultgamemode [mode]`: the mode of new players
- `/tick freeze` and `/tick unfreeze`: the world stands still (time of day, creatures, liquids, plants, machines, hunger), players keep moving; `/tick step [ticks]` moves a frozen world on
- `/tick rate <1..100>` or `/tick rate reset` (20 per second): how fast the world runs
- `/tick sprint <ticks|seconds s|minutes min|hours h|days d>`: run that much game time as fast as possible (`/tick sprint stop` ends it)
- `/tick query`: rate, measured rate, freezing and the time per tick phase
Rate and freezing are not saved; a world always opens normally.
- `/forceload add <x> <z> [<x2> <z2>]`, `/forceload remove <x> <z> [<x2> <z2>]|all`, `/forceload query [<x> <z>]`: keeps chunks loaded although no player is near them (block coordinates of a chunk or of the corners of an area, at most 256 chunks at once and `forcedChunks` in the world), lets them go or lists them; circuits connected to a forced chunk stay loaded with it

**Items, creatures and armour**

- `/give <item> [count]`, `/spawnitem <item> [count]`
- `/summon <creature> [count]` (at most 64 awake summoned creatures, server limit `summonLimit`; creatures of the world do not count), `/kill`, `/heal`, `/feed [points]`
- `/equip <set|armour|clear>` puts on armour (`/equip iron`)
- `/summon armor_stand [property=value …]` and `/armorstand <nearest|looking|id> <property> [values]` place and change armour stands. Properties: `pose <head|body|left_arm|right_arm|left_leg|right_leg> <x> <y> <z>` (degrees), `rotation <degrees>`, `arms`, `plate`, `small`, `showname` with `true|false`, `name [text]`, `equip <set|item>`, `cape <cape|none>`, `clear`, `reset`; `info` lists them. Stand ids are only valid until the world is loaded again; scripts use `nearest` or `looking`. In `/summon` values follow `=` separated by commas: `/summon armor_stand equip=iron pose=right_arm,90,0,0 small`.

**Commands from mods**

Mods add commands as macros (`commands/*.json`): a name and command lines that run one after another with your rights, so a macro never lets you do more than you may do anyway. `/help` lists them with the others.

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-multiplayer"></a>Multiplayer

**Joining a server**

Choose **Multiplayer** in the main menu, type the server's address and your player name and click **Join**. An address is `host`, `host:port`, `192.168.1.20:25601` or `[IPv6 address]:port`; without a port the game uses 25600. Give the password only if the server has one. While the game connects and logs in you can cancel; if the server refuses you, the screen says why (wrong password, full server, other mods, a name that belongs to someone else, a ban). Servers you joined appear under **Recent servers**: a click puts the address into the field, **Join** joins at once, **×** removes the entry. Passwords are never saved.

**Opening your world to the LAN**

In a single-player world, open the menu (Esc) and choose **Open to LAN**. Pick a port (0 takes any free one), an optional password and how many players may join, then **Open to other players**. The screen shows the port and the addresses of your computer; friends in the same network join with one of them. You keep playing as before and stay the world's operator; **Stop hosting** or leaving the world closes it again. The firewall may ask once whether QtCraft may accept incoming connections.

**Dedicated server and the internet**

A server without a window runs with `qtcraft-headless --server`; its settings (name, message of the day, port, password, player limit, world, view distance, operators) are in `server.json` in its data folder, written at the first start. Players over the internet need the server's port forwarded in the router to the server computer, or a server with a public address; QtCraft uses no relay servers.

**Other players**

Hold **Tab** to see who is online: the server's name and message of the day, every player with operators marked and your own name highlighted. The chat tells when somebody joins or leaves. If the connection ends, the game says why; **Join again** connects to the same server, **Back to menu** leaves. In the menu (Esc) of a network game, **Disconnect** leaves the server; the server keeps your inventory and position.

**Limits of the security**

There are no accounts: every installation has a random key that proves your name on each server (the first player to use a name on a server keeps it). Passwords are never sent in plain text, but nothing else is encrypted, so chat and play can be read on the way. Open a server to the internet only with a password and to people you know.

### <a id="chapter-qtcraft-texture_packs"></a>Texture packs

**Choosing a look**

**Settings → Texture packs** changes how blocks, items, creatures, your character and the icons look. **QtCraft HD** paints the game's own textures at 32, 64 or 128 pixels with finer detail; **Storybook** is a small pack of drawn images with outlines and warm colours. Changes apply at once, also in a running world, and only on your computer: other players keep their own choice.

**Several packs at once**

Switch on up to 16 packs. A pack you switch on goes to the top; the arrows change the order. For every texture the highest pack that has it wins, then the next one, and finally the game's own texture, so a small pack can change just a few blocks.

**Your own packs**

A pack is a folder with a **texturepack.json** and PNG images (or texture descriptions) in **textures/qtcraft/**, one per texture, all of the same size from 16 to 512 pixels. **Open texture pack folder** in the settings shows where they go; **Reload** reads them again after a change. Mods can bring packs of their own.

**When something is wrong**

A damaged image or one of the wrong size is skipped and listed under its pack; that texture then comes from the next pack. Very large packs are shown at a lower resolution when the graphics card or the memory would not take them, and the settings say so.
